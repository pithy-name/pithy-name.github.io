Deterministic Redaction Tooling

Pseudonymization for a decade of personal working notes — with no language model anywhere in the loop, because the redactor must not leak what it redacts.

Repository coming soon — being generalized from the private pipeline for public release.

The problem

I wanted AI leverage over ten years of professional notes — 1:1s, reviews, hiring records, incident retros. Those notes are full of other people. Names, initials, email handles, workplace specifics: none of it belongs anywhere near a third-party model, and "the model probably won't repeat it" is not a privacy model.

The tempting shortcut is to ask an LLM to do the scrubbing. That's circular: you'd be uploading the sensitive corpus to protect the sensitive corpus. The redaction layer has to sit below the AI line, or the line is fiction.

The design

  • Deterministic find-and-replace, zero model involvement. The scan matches first names, initial-forms, and email handles — not just exact usernames — because people are referenced the way people talk, not the way databases key them.
  • Stable pseudonym codes. The same person maps to the same code across every one of thousands of files, so the redacted corpus stays analyzable: a coaching arc across three years of 1:1s is still one legible thread, just coded.
  • Paths are identifiers too. Directory names, vault structure, a workplace-named folder — all treated as PII. Redaction that stops at prose leaks through the filesystem.
  • Local re-hydration only. The code→name mapping never leaves the machine. Cloud sees codes; only local, deterministic tooling can translate back.

Proof it holds up

The pipeline has scrubbed thousands of real notes feeding a live retrieval system — the pseudonymized corpus is what makes Scriptorium's model-facing layer possible at all. Every outbound artifact from that system passes a privacy grep gate keyed on the same identifier list before it ships anywhere.
