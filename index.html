About

The systems beneath the symptoms

Most organizational problems don't arrive with clear labels. They show up as missed expectations, recurring friction, duplicated effort, conflicting priorities — the lingering sense that everyone is working hard while something still isn't working. The visible problem is rarely the whole problem.

I'm a people manager and systems thinker who works at the intersection of team leadership, organizational clarity, and execution. I build the connective tissue between Engineering, Product, and the people who depend on what they ship — designing processes and frameworks that compound across every team they touch, rather than solving one problem at a time.

My perspective comes from years spent building, improving, and leading technical teams inside growing technology organizations. I've built functions from scratch, written the leveling frameworks that broader organizations adopted, and created structures that outlived the org charts that housed them. I led through a reorg that eliminated my department — and the systems I'd built kept running.

I'm less interested in management trends than in enduring principles. I believe deeply in multiplicative effects: the right framework or unblock doesn't help one person, it compounds across everyone it touches. A manager shapes a working life. That's not a small thing.

The furthest upstream I can stand and still do what I'm genuinely great at is right here: making the people doing the work as supported and as productive as they can possibly be. Obviously I'd rather build that engine than anything else.

Principles

What I believe about organizations

01

Sustainable performance requires reducing ambiguity, not increasing urgency. Clarity is usually more impactful than additional effort.

02

Leadership is the design of conditions, not the control of people. You cannot steer what you don't understand — and most leaders understand their teams less than they think.

03

Most organizational friction is an incentive problem expressed as a process failure. Fix the structure; the behavior follows.

04

Psychological safety and accountability are co-dependent, not in tension. A team that can't name its problems can't solve them.

05

A manager shapes a working life about as much as almost anyone in it. That's a design responsibility — and it deserves to be taken seriously.

06

The goal isn't to leave a team dependent on you. It's to leave the organization more legible than you found it — and the people in it more capable than when you arrived.

Work

What I've built and shaped

  • Building technical functions from scratch

    Stood up a cross-functional technical function that didn't exist — no template, no line authority, no playbook. Defined what it should own, how it should sit across Engineering, Product, and Support, and how to measure it. The systems I built outlived my direct involvement.

  • Developing people, not just performance

    Spearheaded the engineering career-leveling framework — ran the company-wide brainstorm and captured structured feedback from 40+ engineers on the skills and values it measured. Coached engineers from uncertainty to confidence, with four direct reports full-cycle: technical development, calibrated feedback, performance tied to a ladder I helped write.

  • Creating organizational legibility

    Designed and ran 24 recurring cross-functional programs over 2.6 years — 625 participant-sessions across 18 teams and functions — building the source-of-truth systems that let Engineering, Product, and Support work from the same reality. Documentation as UX: every section answers "why am I here?" and "what do I do next?"

  • Leading through structural ambiguity

    Built through a reorg that eliminated the department housing my team. Maintained continuity, kept systems running, and preserved the working relationships that mattered. The org chart changed; the work didn't break.

  • Multiplicative systems thinking

    The common thread across all of it: designing for compounding rather than addition. A framework that helps ten teams doesn't help ten times as much as one that helps one — it helps more. I build for that leverage.

Selected Work

Real artifacts, not adjectives

A few things I've built recently or built long enough ago that they've proven themselves. Some are code, some are frameworks — all of them exist and run.

Personal knowledge OS · 2026

Scriptorium

A local-first knowledge OS over 10,000+ personal notes: multi-vault ingest, deterministic redaction, and a hybrid keyword-plus-semantic retrieval (RAG) layer — designed so the language model only ever sees what it's explicitly allowed to see. The privacy boundary is the architecture, not an afterthought. It's also where I do my hands-on work with embeddings, retrieval quality, and evaluation — the tooling side of the interpretability world I'm moving toward.

Privacy tooling · 2026

Deterministic redaction pipeline

Pseudonymization tooling that scrubbed thousands of professional notes — names, initials, identifying paths — with deterministic find-and-replace rather than an LLM, so the redaction itself never leaks what it redacts. Built because "trust me, the model probably won't repeat it" is not a privacy model.

notion-to-obsidian
