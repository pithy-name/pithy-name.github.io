About

The systems beneath the symptoms

Draft bio — pending review

Jane of all trades... employed by none.

I spent over a decade as the first engineer to build and run a quality function at a fast-moving startup in a fast & ambiguous product environment where the ground shifted weekly. Instead of waiting for a stable environment, I built flexible frameworks that flexed rigor to the moment, a team of generalists who could take on anything, and a determination that unresolved chaos from above stops with me.

The bigger bet I'm making now: AI Safety and risk mitigation is fundamentally a trust problem; trust is fundamentally an understanding problem; and understanding is what mechanistic interpretability research is building — which is why I'm eager to dedicate the next stage of my career to supporting researchers and enabling them to do what only they can do.

Work

What I've built and shaped

  • Building technical functions from scratch

    Stood up a cross-functional technical function that didn't exist — no template, no playbook (except the ones I wrote). Defined what it should own, how it should sit across Engineering, Product, and Support, and how to measure it. The systems I built outlived my direct involvement.

  • Developing people, not just performance

    Spearheaded the engineering career-leveling framework — ran the company-wide brainstorm and captured structured feedback from 40+ engineers on the skills and values it measured. Coached engineers from uncertainty to confidence, with four direct reports full-cycle: technical development, calibrated feedback, performance tied to a ladder I helped write.

  • Creating organizational legibility

    Designed and ran 24 recurring cross-functional programs — 625 participant-sessions across 18 teams and functions — building the source-of-truth systems that let Engineering, Product, and Support work from the same reality. Documentation as UX: every section answers "why am I here?" and "what do I do next?"

  • Founding culture and inclusion from zero

    Founded and ran the company's first employee resource group — a women's, cross-functional diversity-and-inclusion organization — with no positional authority. Constituted a volunteer board and a full policy agenda in a single founding session, then grew it into a company-wide vehicle for culture and pay-equity influence: 7+ events in the first half-year, reusable norms and shared vocabulary, external speakers, and community partnerships. It kept running after I left.

  • Multiplicative systems thinking

    The common thread across all of it: designing for compounding rather than addition. I build for that leverage.

Code, tools, and systems

Manager OS · 2026

Scriptorium

A local-first knowledge OS designed around people management: multi-vault ingest, deterministic redaction, and a hybrid keyword-plus-semantic retrieval (RAG) layer. Designed with privacy in mind: the LLM only ever sees what it's explicitly allowed to see. This is also where I do my hands-on work with embeddings, retrieval quality, and evals (the tooling side of the interpretability world I'm moving toward).

Privacy tooling · 2026

Deterministic redaction pipeline

Pseudonymization tooling that scrubbed thousands of professional notes — names, initials, identifying paths — with deterministic find-and-replace rather than an LLM, so the redaction itself never leaks what it redacts. Built because "trust me, this probably won't leak" is not a privacy model.

Open source · MIT

notion-to-obsidian

A public converter that reconstructs Notion's database format — properties and body — as plain, portable markdown: every file accounted for — converted, updated, or explicitly skipped with a reason — no silent drops, idempotent re-runs. The unglamorous discipline that makes bulk operations trustworthy.
